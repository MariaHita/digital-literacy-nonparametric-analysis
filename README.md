## 🌐 Project Overview

Digital technology has transformed how people access information, conduct transactions, and participate in economic activities. However, access to digital technology does not necessarily mean that individuals possess sufficient digital literacy or actively participate in the digital economy.

This project investigates three dimensions of students' engagement with the digital economy:

- **Digital Literacy:** Students' knowledge and ability to understand and use digital technology.
- **Digital Economy Participation:** Students' involvement in digital economic activities.
- **Attitudes toward the Digital Economy:** Students' perceptions and responses to digital economic opportunities.

The study examines relationships among these dimensions and investigates differences based on gender and academic semester.

A quantitative approach is applied using questionnaire data from 45 student respondents. Nonparametric statistical methods are used to analyze the relationships and group differences.

### Project Highlights

- Quantitative and questionnaire-based research
- 45 student respondents
- Three main research variables
- Gender-based comparison using the Mann–Whitney test
- Semester trend analysis using the Jonckheere–Terpstra test
- Association analysis using Spearman's rank correlation

## 🎯 Research Background

The growth of Indonesia's digital economy has created opportunities in e-commerce, financial technology, online services, and other technology-based economic activities.

Students, as frequent users of digital technology, have the potential to participate in and contribute to this ecosystem. Nevertheless, digital literacy, participation, and attitudes may differ between individuals.

Understanding these dimensions can help identify areas where digital education and training may be strengthened.

This research therefore examines whether students' digital economy literacy, participation, and attitudes are associated with one another and whether meaningful differences exist across gender and academic semesters.

## ❓ Research Questions

This project addresses four main questions:

1. What is the level of students' digital literacy in the context of the digital economy?
2. What are students' attitudes toward the digital economy?
3. How actively do students participate in digital economic activities?
4. Are there differences in digital literacy, participation, and attitudes based on gender and academic semester?

## 🔬 Methodology

The study follows a quantitative, descriptive, and correlational approach.

The analysis consists of the following stages:

1. **Questionnaire data collection:** Gather responses from student participants.
2. **Descriptive analysis:** Summarize respondent characteristics and the distributions of the research variables.
3. **Validity assessment:** Examine questionnaire item validity using rank-based correlation.
4. **Correlation analysis:** Assess associations among digital literacy, participation, and attitudes.
5. **Gender comparison:** Test for differences between male and female respondents.
6. **Semester trend analysis:** Investigate whether the research variables show an ordered trend across semesters 2, 4, and 6.
7. **Interpretation:** Translate statistical findings into practical insights for digital education.

## 📈 Statistical Analysis

### 1. Descriptive Statistics

Descriptive statistics are used to summarize the respondent profile and the observed scores for each research variable.

This stage provides an overview of the sample before inferential testing.

### 2. Spearman's Rank Correlation

Spearman's rank correlation is used to evaluate the direction and strength of monotonic relationships between the research variables.

The correlation coefficient is denoted by \(\rho\), with values ranging from -1 to 1.

- A positive coefficient indicates that higher values of one variable tend to accompany higher values of another.
- A negative coefficient indicates an inverse association.
- A coefficient near zero indicates a weak monotonic association.

### 3. Mann–Whitney U Test

The Mann–Whitney U test is used to compare the distributions of two independent groups.

In this study, the test examines gender-based differences in:

- Digital literacy
- Digital economy participation
- Attitudes toward the digital economy

### 4. Jonckheere–Terpstra Test

The Jonckheere–Terpstra test is used to assess an ordered trend across independent groups.

The academic semesters are ordered as semester 2, semester 4, and semester 6. The test investigates whether the three research variables show a statistically significant trend across these groups.

The interpretation depends on the specified alternative hypothesis and the corresponding p-value.

## 💡 Key Findings

### 1. Relationship Among Research Variables

Spearman's rank correlation results indicate positive associations among digital literacy, digital economy participation, and attitudes toward the digital economy.

The strongest reported relationship is between digital literacy and digital economy participation:

**Spearman's \(\rho = 0.434\)**

This suggests that students with higher digital literacy scores tend to report greater participation in digital economic activities.

### 2. Gender-Based Differences

According to the report's conclusion, the Mann–Whitney analysis identifies statistically significant differences between gender groups in digital literacy and attitudes toward the digital economy.

The report does not identify a statistically significant gender difference in digital economy participation.

### 3. Differences Across Academic Semesters

The semester trend analysis does not establish a consistent statistically significant increase across all three research variables.

The reported Jonckheere–Terpstra p-values are:

| Variable | Reported p-value | Interpretation at 5% significance level |
|---|---:|---|
| Digital Literacy | 0.042 | Statistically significant trend in the reported two-sided test |
| Digital Economy Participation | 0.194 | No statistically significant trend |
| Attitudes toward the Digital Economy | 0.749 | No statistically significant trend |

**Important interpretation note:** The report's conclusion states that no significant semester trend was found. However, the reported two-sided p-value for digital literacy is 0.042, which is below 0.05. The test direction and calculation should be verified before making a definitive statement about the literacy trend.

## 🌱 Business and Practical Insights

The findings offer several considerations for digital education initiatives.

- **Strengthen digital literacy:** Improving students' digital skills may help support participation in digital economic activities.
- **Promote inclusive digital education:** Training opportunities should be accessible to students across different semesters and backgrounds.
- **Encourage practical engagement:** Digital literacy programs can incorporate practical activities involving online transactions, digital entrepreneurship, and responsible technology use.
- **Evaluate learning outcomes:** Educational institutions can assess whether digital training improves students' knowledge, attitudes, and participation over time.

These recommendations are potential applications of the findings, not causal effects established by this study.

## ⚠️ Limitations

Several limitations should be considered when interpreting the results:

- **Limited sample size:** The analysis includes 45 respondents, which may limit generalizability.
- **Questionnaire-based data:** Responses may be affected by self-reporting and response bias.
- **Potential selection bias:** Google Forms distribution may not represent all students equally.
- **Unequal group sizes:** The number of respondents varies across academic semesters.
- **Cross-sectional design:** The results describe observed relationships and group differences, rather than proving causality or changes over time.

Future research could use a larger and more representative sample, refine the questionnaire, and investigate additional factors associated with students' digital economy engagement.

## ✅ Conclusion

This project applies nonparametric statistical methods to examine students' digital literacy, participation, and attitudes toward the digital economy.

The reported findings indicate a positive relationship between digital literacy and participation, with a Spearman correlation coefficient of 0.434. The report also identifies gender-based differences in digital literacy and attitudes, while participation does not show a significant gender difference in the reported analysis.

The semester trend results require verification, particularly for digital literacy, before a final conclusion is drawn.

Overall, the project demonstrates how descriptive statistics, rank correlation, and nonparametric hypothesis testing can be used to investigate real-world survey data and support evidence-informed educational recommendations.
